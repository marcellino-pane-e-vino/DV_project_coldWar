The Olympic Cold War

Roberto Lazzarini · Project author

Two superpowers, one global stage

From the Soviet Union's Summer Olympic debut in 1952 to its final Summer Games in 1988, Olympic competition became one of the most visible arenas in which the United States and the USSR measured national prestige. This project connects medal performance, sporting specialization and verified direct encounters with the wider geopolitical context of the Cold War.

The primary analytical track below is deliberately Summer-only. Moscow 1980 and Los Angeles 1984 are treated as boycotts and therefore as non-participation, never as zero-performance editions.

Data, processing and limitations

Data Sources

Olympic medal and participation data are derived from the repository's athlete-level 120 years of Olympic history snapshot and its reproducible Summer-only medal preprocessing. Verified USA–USSR direct encounters come from the repository's Olympedia scraper/cache pipeline and retain match-level Olympedia URLs and identifiers. Historical sovereign-state geometry is generated from CShapes 2.0 / the Gleditsch–Ward state system. The nuclear stockpile series is a committed Our World in Data snapshot based on Federation of American Scientists estimates, containing United States and Russia annual values; for the historical 1945–1991 context, the project explicitly treats the source's Russia series as the USSR series and preserves that provenance in preprocessing.

Data Cleaning and Imputation

The Cold War Olympic scope is Summer Games 1952–1988. Team medals are deduplicated at event/delegation/medal level so a team award counts once rather than once per athlete. Missing medal performance is not imputed. A participating delegation with no medal receives zero medal counts; a boycotting superpower is instead represented as non-participation. World Stage includes participant rows with zero medals where a historical sovereign-state mapping is available.

Data Processing and Analysis

Raw data→Cleaning→Transformation→Analysis / metrics→Visualization-ready data→D3 visual encoding

A shared Cold War preprocessing layer produces a canonical intermediate dataset, then one chart-specific notebook generates each runtime CSV. World Stage uses TotalMedalShare = country total medals / all medal awards in that edition and GoldMedalShare = country gold medals / all gold awards in that edition. The color domains are fixed across 1952–1988 separately for the two metrics.

Sporting Fronts: the visualization is cumulative and has no per-edition selector. Moscow 1980 and Los Angeles 1984 are excluded because the analytical question is specialization when both superpowers were present; the cumulative comparison therefore combines 1952, 1956, 1960, 1964, 1968, 1972, 1976 and 1988 only. Its head-to-head scope still measures medal difference, but restricts the cumulative sport comparison to sports supported by verified direct-encounter evidence.

Rivalry Pulse: precision is preferred to recall. Every dot must have reliable match/bout-level evidence of a binary USA–USSR contest. The scraper output preserves source URLs, raw stage labels and defensibly normalized stage labels. Where the validated intermediate data contain numeric scores for both sides, those scores are exposed in the visualization tooltip; non-numeric result methods are not converted into artificial scores. Doubtful or inferred encounters are excluded.

Who Won — Battle Strip: Local Ripple: edition-level medal totals use a square-root margin scale, local-repulsion layout, focus interaction and USA/USSR donut values. The expanded surface uses a dark tint of the edition winner's color; boycott editions remain non-comparable methodological markers.

Limitations

The direct-encounter dataset is intentionally conservative and is limited to the sports currently covered by the validated scraper; additional binary-format disciplines can be added in a later scraper version. Medal-share denominators reflect medal awards represented by the underlying Olympic dataset after team-award deduplication. Historical delegation-to-state mapping excludes non-sovereign/composite delegations where a valid CShapes sovereign geometry is not appropriate. The nuclear Russia→USSR interpretation is a project convention for this source snapshot and is documented rather than silently applied in the frontend.
